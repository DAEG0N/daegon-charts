Daegon 100 vs. Top 100 Albums vs. Artist 50: what each chart is measuring

Three flagship rankings, three different questions: which songs led the week, which albums accumulated the strongest consumption, and which artists had the broadest overall presence.

By Daegon Charts Editorial · October 13, 2026

Daegon’s three flagship weekly rankings are related, but they are not interchangeable. The Daegon 100 ranks songs. Top 100 Albums aggregates album-level performance. Artist 50 measures the artist’s wider weekly footprint. Reading the three together reveals whether a week is driven by one enormous single, a deep album, or a catalog-wide surge.

Daegon 100: the song competition

The Daegon 100 asks which individual tracks performed strongest in the selected week under the chart’s current consumption model. Each song owns its position and historical run. Album context can explain why multiple tracks rise together, but the songs still compete separately.

Top 100 Albums: depth matters

An album can be strong because one track is enormous, but it can also rise because many tracks contribute at once. That distinction is visible in weeks with an “album bomb,” when a new release sends several tracks onto the songs chart simultaneously. The album ranking captures the combined effect at the project level rather than treating every track as an isolated event.

Artist 50: the broadest view

Artist 50 is designed to show the artist’s total weekly presence. A performer with several songs and albums active at once can outrank someone with one giant song but little activity elsewhere. The artist ranking therefore answers a different question from the Daegon 100: not “what is the biggest song?” but “who has the strongest overall week?”

Same week, different leader?That can be completely valid because the charts aggregate at different levels.

October 10, 2026 is a clean example

Ariana Grande leads all three flagship rankings at once. “hate that i made you love me” is No. 1 on the Daegon 100, petal is No. 1 on Top 100 Albums, and Grande is No. 1 on Artist 50. That alignment shows strength at the track, album and artist levels simultaneously.

But the rest of the lists diverge quickly. Taylor Swift is No. 2 on Artist 50 and No. 2 with The Life of a Showgirl on albums, while her highest song that week is “Patient Zero” at No. 7. Olivia Rodrigo is No. 3 on Artist 50 and No. 3 on albums, while her highest song is No. 9. A reader who looked only at songs would miss the scale of their broader weekly activity.

Why this structure is useful for historical research

Separating the levels prevents one metric from pretending to answer every question. Song history is ideal for tracking hits. Album history shows eras and deep consumption. Artist history helps identify periods of sustained dominance. Together they create a more complete archive than a single all-purpose ranking.
